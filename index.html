Choose a traceable model
The recommended entry contains six ILLUSTRATIVE engineering estimates (80/86/90/94/96/98% HHV) constructed from the pinned Crest table and explicit project assumptions. These are scenarios for illustration and preliminary comparisons, not verified manufacturer performance maps. The published Crest family table is under Data; the CT3-17 curve is separately listed as an OpenStudio reference fit with original equipment data unverified. Obtain equipment-specific manufacturer data and fit it whenever possible. Generic OpenStudio templates are archived in the source snapshot and are omitted from the interactive catalog.
Active model: reference, limits and export
Read source and export separately
Source calculation is available for every record: it evaluates a published polynomial, NECB rational relationship or interpolated Crest table. For the recommended project scenarios, the button is labelled Illustrative model and evaluates the table-based engineering surrogate. Changing a category, model or calculation keeps the selected chart layout, including Three Chart Dashboard. Temperature controls are disabled only when the source has no temperature variable. E+ derived fit is shown only when the IDF needs a separate software-generated approximation; a published EnergyPlus polynomial already serves as both source and export. Both efficiency and factor views respect the selected calculation. Fit domains and validation errors appear in Help and the IDF header.
NECB: three calculations, two different comparisons
Source calculation evaluates NECB fuel ratio F directly and converts it to efficiency: η = ηnom × p/F. For 2025 condensing, F uses TF = 1.8TC + 32. Absolute efficiency depends on the adopted nominal reference; this step does not refit the formula. E+ derived fit approximates p/F with an efficiency-modifier polynomial over the stated fit domain. The rational function generally cannot be represented exactly by six biquadratic coefficients; the current coefficient export therefore requires a fit. Physical reference is evaluated independently from its own empirical temperature, load and cycling assumptions, not from NECB or its E+ fit. Source versus fit measures export approximation error; source versus reference compares model responses and does not establish NECB physical error. IDF always exports the source-based fit, including while Physical reference is selected.
NECB shared inferred operating envelope
For NECB 2020/2025 condensing, the same switch controls the inferred operating conditions in all three calculation views. Project assumptions: supply 60–82°C, water ΔT = 16p K, output PLR 0.25–1; return boundaries are 60−16p and 82−16p°C. This is a system scenario, not a normative curve validity domain or validated equipment range. The common footprint is clipped to the source and current plot domains; switching to Physical reference does not extend it. Source and fit use a shaded patch and boundary; Physical reference uses only the same amber boundary. Each height is calculated by the selected model. Shared conditions do not imply NECB calibration or endorsement of the reference. The switch does not change calculations, coefficients or IDF fit bounds.
Illustrative family: progressive heat transfer
The seed is the pinned LBL Crest HHV table. Both dry sensible recovery and the normalized condensing-associated residual improve across 90/94/96/98% scenarios. A bounded effectiveness-inspired transformation uses a 90% HHV dry-recovery ceiling and relative sensible index (scenario reference efficiency / Crest reference efficiency)³; exponent 3 is an explicit engineering assumption. A separate recovery index is calibrated at 26.629°C/full firing. These indices are not measured UA or exchanger area. The four default surfaces remain distinct in both cold and hot operation; polynomial exports include a checked ordering constraint. The 80/86% cases remain weaker dry-branch assumptions. HHV efficiency is bounded at 100%; this does not certify equipment accuracy. Use Compare all four efficiency surfaces to inspect the full family and download its model data. Its 3D tracers follow both sliders: high-contrast solid lines show fixed-PLR slices, red dashed lines show fixed-temperature slices, and dots mark their intersections. The 2D charts place Partial Load on the left and RWT on the right; vertical dashed guides, value labels and intersection dots mark the current selection. Show traces toggles the 3D tracers and 2D selection guides, labels and dots together; their heights match the selected model or export fit without offsets.
High-temperature planning assumption
At full firing and a return temperature of about 71°C, this model gives about 85–88% HHV across the four scenarios, with smaller differences at the hot end. Without equipment data, an early-stage high-temperature assumption of about 86% HHV (efficiency conversion: η_LHV ≈ η_HHV × 1.11, or about 95% LHV for the assumed natural-gas heat-value ratio) can be explored with upward and downward variations of 1–2 percentage points for sensitivity analysis. Use manufacturer data for the corresponding conditions when available; small differences between adjacent scenarios have not been validated against equipment data and should not be overinterpreted.
Nominal efficiency and units
The software presents efficiencies on an HHV basis. For CT3-17, HHV follows the EnergyPlus field convention used by the upstream application code; the original analysis basis remains unverified. See the active-model evidence note. Efficiency factors are dimensionless; they can coincide or cross even when absolute efficiencies differ. Nominal efficiency is a model reference, not a seasonal AFUE or combustion-efficiency value. Changing it proportionally scales the selected curve, creating a user scenario; it does not regenerate a different equipment design or preserve the default family-order guarantee. Crest normalization is locked to preserve the original table. Independent Physical reference changes do not rescale its locked source export. SI/IP changes display units; inlet temperature is converted back to °C for calculation.
Charts and exports
The charts use output PLR (useful heat / reference useful heat). Controls use the selected model domain. Published polynomials and table estimates hold inputs at their declared boundaries; NECB source formulas reject nonpositive fuel fractions. A displayed or fitted envelope does not establish equipment performance outside that envelope. CSV contains the selected calculation, source link, HHV efficiency and modifier. IDF uses the published curve or labelled derived fit; inspect its envelope before integrating the node names into a plant.

Open the standalone Community help. The release includes a Chinese user guide, method, data-source notes and release notes in docs/. The main HTML contains its Help tabs; the four-tier dashboard and its CSV are companion files that must be kept beside it.
Network and licenses
The HTML requires network access for Plotly and fonts. Complete imported licenses are embedded in the Copyright & Licenses tab and shipped with the release.
Efficiency modifier versus fuel ratio
EnergyPlus uses η = ηnom × f(p,T). A modifier of 1.03 means 3% above the nominal reference, not 103% absolute efficiency. A Cubic curve is C₁ + C₂p + C₃p² + C₄p³. A Biquadratic curve is C₁ + C₂p + C₃p² + C₄T + C₅T² + C₆pT.
NECB independent calculation
The reference relationship describes fuel fraction F, giving f = p/F. The 2020 condensing and reference non-condensing/modulating implementations are one-dimensional. The 2025 condensing relationship uses Fahrenheit (°F): TF = 1.8TC + 32 inside F. Nonpositive fuel fractions are invalid; the software does not replace them with an arbitrary positive floor.
Estimated four-tier family and hot-end values
The nominal 90/94/96/98% values share an assumed 26.629°C/full-firing anchor. Their full-firing estimates at about 71°C are approximately 85/86.5/87.5/88% HHV, rounded to 0.5 percentage points for explanation. Tables retain the two-decimal model outputs; those digits do not indicate equipment prediction accuracy. The ordering follows this model’s assumptions, and 85–88% is not a universal equipment range or lower bound. The default estimate uses output PLR 0.25–0.85 and inlet 21.024–71.130°C. The 80/86% assumptions use only 65.593–71.130°C. The full-firing anchor is not a claim that output PLR 1 is available at every temperature.
Bounded recovery transformation
Let S be the interpolated Crest efficiency and D the dry branch extrapolated from its two hottest nodes. Set c=max[0,(S−D)/(1−D)], H=0.90 and ks=(ηanchor/ηCrest,ref)³. Then D′=H[1−(1−D/H)ks], c′=1−(1−c)kc, and η′=D′+(1−D′)c′. Solve kc at the common anchor. This effectiveness-inspired surrogate changes both recovery components; it is not a first-principles solution for exchanger geometry or actual UA.
Crest load definition
The original table has inlet temperatures in kelvin and fuel firing fractions r = 0.05, 0.5, 1.0. The page converts temperature to °C and uses bilinear interpolation. To express useful-output PLR, it solves p = r × η(T,r)/ηref. ηref = 0.962656903765690 at 26.629381°C and full firing is a normalization choice, not a separately certified rating. For the unmodified Crest table, the plot/fit domain p = 0.25–0.90 stays within the shared table domain and above the minimum firing rates of all six models.
Derived fit and error
NECB p/F and the interpolated Crest data are not exact six-coefficient polynomials. SimBoiler computes OLS fits over explicitly stated envelopes and validates them on a denser offset grid. RMSE and maximum error are reported in absolute efficiency percentage points, at the source nominal efficiency. Changing nominal efficiency scales errors proportionally only where output clipping is unchanged; otherwise the error must be reassessed. The estimated four-tier polynomials additionally receive a joint bounded coefficient projection, with continuous-domain checks for a minimum 0.1 pp tier gap and a 99.9% HHV upper bound at default nominal values. Their checked maximum-error budget is 2.1 pp against the estimate, including separate boundary sampling. Fit bounds are software choices, not normative requirements.
OpenStudio CT3-17 arithmetic check
At inlet 71.13°C and output PLR 1, the published fit gives f = 0.9132550984; 0.962 × f = 87.8551%. This is a published-model cross-check under the EnergyPlus HHV convention, not independent equipment validation or verification of the original rating anchor. Output PLR 1 is nominal useful heat output, not necessarily full fuel firing. The raw JSON labels the dependent variable FIRRatio, but upstream code assigns this polynomial directly as the normalized boiler efficiency curve.
High-temperature evidence coverage
Crest endpoint data and the OpenStudio CT3-17 fit provide a magnitude comparison near 88%; they do not independently validate the four-tier model. The lower 84.96% and 86.62% scenarios lack independent equipment evidence. At inlet 71.13°C and output PLR 1, the EnergyPlus example gives 0.89 × 0.9975563812 = 88.7825% HHV, so it does not corroborate the lower tiers. It is a typical example citing Viessmann literature, not a verified low-efficiency product. Holding the CT3 fit fixed and hypothetically changing its nominal multiplier to 0.981 gives 89.5903%, a 1.7352 pp increase; this is sensitivity only, not a justified replacement or uncertainty interval. The available evidence does not establish the dominant source of uncertainty.
High-temperature mechanism and uncertainty
At high return temperatures, the condensing contribution diminishes and sensible heat recovery and other losses dominate. Higher part-load efficiency can occur during stable modulation; very-low-load cycling losses need separate consideration. The 1–2 pp variations are analysis scenarios, not a measured uncertainty interval. RMSE and maximum errors quantify the export polynomial versus the estimated model, not the model versus real equipment.
Physical interpretation
The approximate 54°C dew-point marker is context only; it is not a universal boiler operating limit or an extra efficiency correction. Physical reference is an independent empirical model with a dew-point transition, part-load response and assumed cycling penalty: nominal anchor 26.7°C at PLR 1, with a 100% HHV upper bound. It is available for applicable published condensing comparison models, but hidden for the recommended illustrative family. It is not measured manufacturer data or a validation of the source curve. IDF continues to export the source polynomial or derived source fit; locked Crest source normalization is retained. A numeric efficiency range check does not certify a model or establish code compliance.
Catalog provenance

Each row links to the pinned upstream record or official NECB publication. For Crest, the LBL source explicitly uses NaturalGasHigherHeatingValue; the manufacturer chart itself does not label HHV/LHV. For CT3-17, the OpenStudio JSON attributes the analysis to Kamel Haddad but does not link the original samples or report, establish the rating anchor, or explicitly confirm the heat-value basis. Its 96.2% is retained as the upstream nominal reference, not a verified manufacturer rating. Local upstream copies and checks are documented in docs/Community_Source_Verification.md. The former authority claims for AHRI/NREL/T24/ASHRAE/EU coefficients were not established and have been withdrawn. The recommended scenarios are now generated from the pinned Crest table with documented decomposition and calibration assumptions. Original empirical scenarios are retained only as historical metadata. The generic OpenStudio templates remain in the source archive.
